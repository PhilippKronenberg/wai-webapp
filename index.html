What moved the nowcast this week

How each indicator relates to the index

The index is the common movement of all its input series. R² is the share of a series' own variation that this common movement explains, from 0 to 1. The loading is how strongly the series moves with the index, with its 95% credible interval; series are standardised first, so loadings are comparable across series.

About the index

The WAI extracts a single dynamic factor from weekly, monthly and quarterly Swiss indicators. Missing and mixed-frequency observations are handled by data augmentation, the state equation carries stochastic volatility so crisis periods are not smoothed away, and the factor's loading on GDP is fixed at one — which is what makes the index interpretable as a GDP growth rate rather than an abstract index.

Why two WAI lines on the growth charts. The blue line is the weekly index: an instantaneous annualised growth rate, read every week. Quarterly GDP is a flow — the average level of activity across the whole quarter — so the like-for-like comparison is the green series, the weekly index averaged over each quarter. The difference is not cosmetic. Across 2020Q2 activity collapsed and largely recovered inside the quarter, so the weekly rate ends the quarter near zero while the quarterly average is about −23% annualised, which is what GDP reported. Compared correctly, the WAI matches published GDP with a correlation of 1.000 and a root-mean-square error of 0.04 percentage points across 143 quarters.

Reading the contributions panel. The decomposition is additive and exact: for any week the pieces sum to that week's quarter-on-quarter growth rate, in percentage points of it. Seven of the pieces are groups of input indicators — surveys, financial markets, alternative data such as traffic and card payments, prices, retail, trade, and published GDP itself. The other two are not data groups and are shown in grey. Trend is the growth the model implies with every data contribution set to zero, so it is roughly average growth and barely moves: about 1.6 points, week after week. Estimation uncertainty (parameter_uncertainty in the published file) is the gap between the posterior-mean index and this plug-in decomposition; it is small in quiet periods and can be sizeable in volatile ones, and it is shown rather than quietly spread over the other pieces.

Why GDP dominates the early years. The factor is anchored to GDP, and the weekly and monthly indicators only begin to arrive from around 2019. Before that, almost the whole index is the GDP (target) piece, because official GDP is almost the only thing informing it. That is the model working as intended rather than a fault in the chart; the data groups become visible from 2019 and dramatically so through 2020.

Reading the news panel. Every update re-estimates the nowcast of this quarter's GDP growth with the data released since the previous one. Before a release, the model has an expectation of what it will show; the news is the gap between the release and that expectation, and the panel shows how much each group's news moved the nowcast, in percentage points of annualised growth. The pieces add up exactly to the change between the two nowcasts, with the model's parameters held at last week's estimate so that only the data differs. A quiet week, with few releases that were roughly as expected, moves the nowcast by thousandths of a point — that is the panel working, not failing.

Reading the series panel. R² says how much of an indicator's own week-to-week variation the common factor explains; a low value is normal for a noisy weekly series and does not mean the series is useless, since the model weighs each one by how informative it is. The loading says how strongly, and in which direction, the series moves with the factor, with a 95% credible interval; an interval that excludes zero means the series reliably co-moves with activity. Published GDP is not charted: its loading is fixed at one, which is what anchors the index to GDP, so its R² of one is a definition rather than a result.

Shaded areas are 95% credible bands from the posterior. They are shown for the growth-rate views only: the level index is a cumulation of the growth rate, and the model does not supply a compounded credible interval for it, so no band is drawn rather than one that would look like a level band without being one.
